# ACM Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct (2018)

> This file is a paraphrased summary for quick lookup, not the Code itself. The
> authoritative text is at https://www.acm.org/code-of-ethics (Copyright (c) 2018 by the
> Association for Computing Machinery). Check the original before quoting.

Quick lookup for citing principles. The public good is always the paramount
consideration. The Code is a basis for ethical decision-making, not an algorithm —
multiple principles often apply at once with differing relevance.

## 1. General Ethical Principles

- **1.1 Contribute to society and human well-being** — Use skills for the benefit of
  society and the environment; promote human rights and autonomy; minimize negative
  consequences (health, safety, security, privacy). When groups' interests conflict,
  prioritize the less advantaged. Promote accessibility and environmental sustainability.
- **1.2 Avoid harm** — "Harm" = significant, unjust negative consequences (physical/mental
  injury, destruction/disclosure of info, damage to property/reputation/environment).
  Unintended harm must be undone/mitigated. Intentional harm must be ethically justified
  and minimized. Follow accepted best practices; analyze data aggregation and emergent
  effects; report system risks (whistleblow if leaders don't act, but assess carefully first).
- **1.3 Be honest and trustworthy** — Full disclosure of capabilities, limitations, and
  problems. No false/misleading claims, fabricated data, or bribes. Be honest about your
  own qualifications and competence limits; disclose conflicts of interest; honor commitments;
  don't misrepresent or speak for an organization without authorization.
- **1.4 Be fair and take action not to discriminate** — Equality, tolerance, respect, justice.
  Provide avenues for redress. No prejudicial discrimination (age, color, disability, ethnicity,
  family status, gender identity, union membership, military status, nationality, race, religion,
  sex, sexual orientation, etc.). Harassment is discrimination. Design for inclusiveness and
  accessibility; failing to do so may itself be unfair discrimination.
- **1.5 Respect the work required to produce new ideas/works/artifacts** — Credit creators;
  respect copyrights, patents, trade secrets, licenses. Don't unduly oppose reasonable uses;
  don't claim private ownership of shared public resources (e.g. FOSS, public domain).
- **1.6 Respect privacy** — Use personal info only for legitimate ends. Prevent re-identification
  and unauthorized collection; ensure accuracy and known provenance; protect from unauthorized
  access. Transparent policies; informed consent; allow review/correction/deletion. Collect only
  the minimum necessary; define retention/disposal; don't repurpose without consent; take special
  care when merging data collections.
- **1.7 Honor confidentiality** — Protect entrusted confidential info (trade secrets, client data,
  strategies, financials, research, pre-publication work). Exception: evidence of a violation of
  law, organizational regulations, or the Code — then disclose only to appropriate authorities.

## 2. Professional Responsibilities

- **2.1 Strive for high quality** in processes and products; respect the dignity of all affected;
  support transparent communication; resist inducements to neglect quality.
- **2.2 Maintain professional competence, conduct, and ethical practice** — technical knowledge
  plus awareness of social context, communication, reflective analysis, and ethical navigation;
  keep upgrading skills.
- **2.3 Know and respect existing rules** (laws, regulations, org policies) unless there is a
  compelling ethical justification otherwise. Challenge unethical rules through existing channels
  first; accept responsibility for any decision to violate one.
- **2.4 Accept and provide appropriate professional review** — seek peer/stakeholder review;
  give constructive critical reviews of others' work.
- **2.5 Give comprehensive evaluations and risk analysis** — objective, credible evaluations and
  testimony. Extraordinary care for ML systems; reassess risk as systems evolve; don't deploy
  systems whose future risks can't be reliably predicted; report major risks.
- **2.6 Perform work only in areas of competence** — evaluate feasibility/advisability; disclose
  lack of expertise; ethical judgment is the final guide on whether to take an assignment.
- **2.7 Foster public awareness and understanding of computing** — share knowledge clearly and
  respectfully; address inaccurate or misleading information about computing.
- **2.8 Access resources only when authorized or compelled by the public good** — public
  accessibility alone is not authorization. Unauthorized access only in exceptional cases (e.g. to
  inhibit malicious systems) with extraordinary precautions.
- **2.9 Design and implement systems that are robustly and usably secure** — security as a primary
  consideration; due diligence; monitoring/patching/vulnerability reporting; timely breach
  notification; usable (not confusing) security. If misuse/harm is predictable or unavoidable, the
  best option may be not to build the system.

## 3. Professional Leadership Principles

("Leader" = anyone with influence, educational, or managerial responsibility.)

- **3.1 Ensure the public good is the central concern** across the whole lifecycle (research →
  design → deployment → maintenance → retirement → disposal).
- **3.2 Articulate, encourage, and evaluate social responsibilities** of the organization/group.
- **3.3 Manage personnel and resources to enhance quality of working life** — development,
  accessibility, safety, psychological well-being, human dignity, ergonomics.
- **3.4 Support policies and processes that reflect the Code** — communicate, reward compliance,
  act on violations; don't design processes that enable Code violations.
- **3.5 Create opportunities for members to grow as professionals** — including awareness of system
  consequences, limitations, the inevitability of software errors, and complexity.
- **3.6 Use care when modifying or retiring systems** — consider impact on dependent users;
  investigate alternatives; assist graceful migration; warn of risks of continued use well ahead.
- **3.7 Recognize and care for systems integrated into societal infrastructure** — heightened
  stewardship; fair access (including for the excluded); monitor adoption level and adjust duties;
  develop standards of care where none exist.

## 4. Compliance with the Code

- **4.1 Uphold, promote, and respect the principles** — resolve recognized breaches, including
  raising concerns with suspected violators when reasonable.
- **4.2 Treat violations as inconsistent with ACM membership** — consider reporting breaches.
